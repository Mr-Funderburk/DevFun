What is Computer Science

"Computer Science is the study of computers and algorithmic processes, their principles, their designs, their applications, and their impact on society."

— Computer Science Teachers Association (CSTA)

Computer Science IS:

  • Theory & Practice: Allows you to program a computer to do what you want.
  • A Storytelling Tool: Helps you express ideas or build solutions with technology.
  • Problem Solving: Emphasizes persistence and logic applicable across all workforce sectors.
  • Active Creation: Teaches students how to create with technology, not just consume it.

Computer Science is NOT:

  • Learning how to type or use a mouse.
  • Learning basic software applications (e.g., Word, PowerPoint, Google Docs, Drive).
  • Learning how to build or repair computer hardware.
  • Simply playing video games.

Computer Science is Not Just Coding

Computer science is not just about building computers or writing computer programs. Computer science is no more about building computers and developing software than astronomy is about building telescopes, biology is about building microscopes, or music is about building instruments.

Key Takeaway: It is about how we use such tools, and what we find out when we do. The solution to many computer science problems may not even require the use of computers — just pencil, paper, and logic. Problems in computer science were tackled decades before physical digital computers were even built.

While CS certainly involves coding, programming is merely the tool we use to express algorithmic solutions to complex problems.

A Brief History of Computer Science

Thanks to computer science, we landed humans on the moon, connected the globe via the internet, and placed portable computers in billions of hands. Here is how it started:

Charles Babbage - 1821

English mathematician who conceived a steam-driven calculating machine called the Difference Engine. Though it failed due to technological limits of the era, it laid early mechanical concepts for computing.

Ada Lovelace - 1848

Recognized as the world's first computer programmer. While translating notes on Babbage’s Analytical Engine, Lovelace added a step-by-step algorithm to calculate Bernoulli numbers, foreseeing that machines could manipulate symbols, not just numbers.

Alan Turing - 1936

Presented the concept of the Turing Machine in his paper "On Computable Numbers...", establishing the foundational theoretical model for all modern digital computers. He also devised the Turing Test for artificial intelligence.

Grace Hopper - 1953

Navy Rear Admiral who revolutionized software by creating early compilers, leading to COBOL (COmmon Business-Oriented Language) — allowing computers to be programmed using human-readable English words rather than machine code.

George Forsythe - 1961

Coined the official academic term "Computer Science", defining it as programming theory, data processing, numerical analysis, and systems design. He later established Stanford's CS department.

Steve Jobs & Steve Wozniak - 1976

Founded Apple Computer out of a garage, sparking the consumer personal computer revolution that brought affordable computing hardware directly into homes and classrooms.

Seven Impacts of Computer Science

01 · GLOBAL CONNECTION

Connects People Anywhere

From instant video chat to streaming, communications tech links families, schools, and workplaces across continents.

02 · DAILY LIFE

Shapes Everyday Choices

Smartphone alarms, GPS navigation maps, social networks, and online shopping influence how we navigate every day.

03 · CYBERSECURITY

Provides Security Solutions

Keeps personal data private, protects public infrastructure like airports, and stops data breaches via ethical hacking.

04 · PUBLIC SAFETY

Saves Lives in Disasters

Predictive weather models and early warning systems allow communities to evacuate before hurricanes or tsunamis strike.

05 · SOCIAL GOOD

Alleviates Societal Issues

Provides tools to model climate change, optimize resource distribution, and run global fundraising for charities.

06 · ADVOCACY

Empowers Public Voices

Gives anyone with computer access a platform to organize social movements, educate communities, and share ideas.

07 · HEALTHCARE

Revolutionizes Modern Medicine

Electronic medical records, computational genomics, and personalized medicine accelerate medical research and improve patient care.

Conclusion Questions

Answer the following questions in full, complete sentences.

  1. In your own words, explain the difference between computer science and coding (programming). Why is coding considered a tool used by computer scientists rather than the entire discipline?
  2. Re-read the quote: "Computer science is no more about computers than astronomy is about telescopes." How does this analogy explain why a computer scientist might solve complex computational problems using only pencil and paper?
  3. Select one pioneer in Computer Science that was not listed in this lesson. What was their major contribution to the field, and why was it revolutionary during their time? (Be ready to present your person to the class.)
  4. Choose two of the Seven Impacts of Computer Science discussed in class. Describe how technology in those areas directly influences your daily life, and identify one ethical responsibility or potential risk developers must consider.
  5. Computer science powers almost every industry on Earth. Research a specific career path in the CS field (Tip: Look for unique roles beyond just "Software Engineer"—e.g., Cybersecurity Analyst, AI Ethics Researcher, Game Engine Developer, or UX Designer).
    • Job Title: Official title of the role.
    • Description: What does this person do day-to-day?
    • Starting Salary: Average entry-level salary range.
    • Requirements: Required degree, certifications, or key technical skills.
    • Reflection: What surprised you or caught your interest about this role?
