Binary & Base Conversion

Master bidirectional conversions between Base-10 (Decimal) and Base-2 (Binary) using positional weighting, doubling, subtraction, and short division.

"There are 10 kinds of people in the world: those who understand binary, and those who don't."

1. The Base-2 (Binary) System

Binary is a base-2 positional number system that uses only two digits: 0 and 1. While human decimal arithmetic operates on powers of ten (100, 101, 102, ...), binary positional values double with each step to the left as increasing powers of two (20, 21, 22, ...).

For AP Computer Science Principles, you should memorize the first 8 positional powers of two from right to left:

Exponent (2n) 27 26 25 24 23 22 21 20
Decimal Value 128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1

To convert a 4-bit binary nibble like 10112 to decimal, multiply each bit by its positional power of 2 and sum the results:

(1 × 23) + (0 × 22) + (1 × 21) + (1 × 20) = 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 1110

2. Converting Binary to Decimal (Base-2 → Base-10)

Method A: Positional Notation (Recommended)

Write out the positional powers of two above each bit. Add together the place values of all columns containing a 1.

Example: Convert the 8-bit binary string 1001 10102 to decimal.

Place Value (2n) 128 (27) 64 (26) 32 (25) 16 (24) 8 (23) 4 (22) 2 (21) 1 (20)
Binary Bit 1 0 0 1 1 0 1 0
Value Added 128 0 0 16 8 0 2 0
128 + 16 + 8 + 2 = 15410

Method B: The Doubling Method

Start from the leftmost bit (Most Significant Bit). Multiply your running total by 2, add the current bit, and repeat for every remaining bit across the string.

doubling_method_trace.txt
Trace 
Target Binary: 10011010

Start total = 0
Step 1 (Bit 1): (0 * 2) + 1 = 1
Step 2 (Bit 0): (1 * 2) + 0 = 2
Step 3 (Bit 0): (2 * 2) + 0 = 4
Step 4 (Bit 1): (4 * 2) + 1 = 9
Step 5 (Bit 1): (9 * 2) + 1 = 19
Step 6 (Bit 0): (19 * 2) + 0 = 38
Step 7 (Bit 1): (38 * 2) + 1 = 77
Step 8 (Bit 0): (77 * 2) + 0 = 154

Final Decimal Result = 154

3. Converting Decimal to Binary (Base-10 → Base-2)

Method A: Descending Powers (Subtraction Method)

Find the largest power of two that fits inside your target decimal number. Subtract that power value, place a 1 in its column, and evaluate the remainder. If a power value is larger than the remaining target, place a 0 in that column and move to the next smaller power.

Example: Convert decimal 20510 to an 8-bit binary string.

  1. 28 = 256 is larger than 205, so we start at 27 = 128.
  2. 205 ≥ 128 → Place 1 in 128 column. Remainder: 205 - 128 = 77.
  3. 77 ≥ 64 → Place 1 in 64 column. Remainder: 77 - 64 = 13.
  4. 13 < 32 → Place 0 in 32 column. Remainder remains 13.
  5. 13 < 16 → Place 0 in 16 column. Remainder remains 13.
  6. 13 ≥ 8 → Place 1 in 8 column. Remainder: 13 - 8 = 5.
  7. 5 ≥ 4 → Place 1 in 4 column. Remainder: 5 - 4 = 1.
  8. 1 < 2 → Place 0 in 2 column. Remainder remains 1.
  9. 1 ≥ 1 → Place 1 in 1 column. Remainder: 1 - 1 = 0.
Column Power (2n) 128 64 32 16 8 4 2 1
Binary Result 1 1 0 0 1 1 0 1
20510 = 1100 11012

Method B: Short Division (Successive Division by 2)

Repeatedly divide your decimal number by 2. Record the integer quotient for the next division step and record the remainder (0 or 1). The binary result is read from bottom to top (the last remainder is the Most Significant Bit).

Example: Convert 20510 using short division.

Division Operation Integer Quotient Remainder Bit Significance
205 ÷ 2 102 1 Least Significant Bit (LSB - Rightmost)
102 ÷ 2 51 0 Bit 1
51 ÷ 2 25 1 Bit 2
25 ÷ 2 12 1 Bit 3
12 ÷ 2 6 0 Bit 4
6 ÷ 2 3 0 Bit 5
3 ÷ 2 1 1 Bit 6
1 ÷ 2 0 1 Most Significant Bit (MSB - Leftmost)

Reading remainders from bottom to top yields: 110011012.
